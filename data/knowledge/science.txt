Photosynthesis is how plants make their own food. Inside chloroplasts, the green pigment chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and uses that energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose, releasing oxygen as a by-product. In short: light + water + carbon dioxide gives sugar + oxygen.

Gravity is the force that pulls objects with mass toward each other. Newton described it as a force that depends on mass and distance, while Einstein's general relativity explains it as the bending of spacetime by mass. On Earth, gravity accelerates falling objects at about 9.8 metres per second squared.

The water cycle is the endless movement of water around the planet. The sun heats oceans and lakes so water evaporates, the vapour cools and condenses into clouds, then falls as rain or snow (precipitation), and flows back into rivers and seas where it is collected again.

Atoms are the building blocks of matter. Each atom has a nucleus made of protons and neutrons, with electrons moving around it. The number of protons decides which element the atom is, for example one proton is hydrogen and six protons is carbon.

Electricity is the flow of electric charge, usually electrons moving through a conductor such as copper wire. Voltage is the push that drives the charge, current is how much charge flows, and resistance opposes the flow. Ohm's law links them: voltage equals current times resistance.

Evolution by natural selection was described by Charles Darwin in his 1859 book On the Origin of Species. Living things vary, and those with traits that help them survive and reproduce pass those traits on, so helpful traits become more common over many generations.

Matter commonly exists as a solid, a liquid or a gas, and a fourth state called plasma exists in stars and lightning. Heating a substance adds energy so its particles move faster, which is why ice melts into water and water boils into steam.

Vaccines train the immune system without causing the full disease. They show the body a harmless piece or a weakened form of a germ, so the immune system makes antibodies and memory cells that can respond quickly if the real germ shows up later.
