The Solar System has eight planets orbiting the Sun. In order from the Sun they are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. The first four are small rocky planets, Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, and Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.

The Sun is a star about 4.6 billion years old that holds 99.8 percent of the mass of the Solar System. It shines because of nuclear fusion in its core, where hydrogen atoms fuse into helium and release huge amounts of energy.

The Moon orbits Earth about every 27.3 days at an average distance of 384,400 kilometres. Its gravity causes ocean tides. Apollo 11 landed the first humans on the Moon on 20 July 1969, with Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walking on the surface.

A black hole is a region of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape once it crosses the event horizon. Many form when a very massive star collapses at the end of its life. The first image of a black hole, M87 star, was released in 2019 by the Event Horizon Telescope.

Mars looks red because its soil is rich in iron oxide, or rust. It has Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the Solar System, and a day about 24 hours and 37 minutes long. NASA rovers such as Curiosity and Perseverance are exploring its surface.

India's Chandrayaan-3 mission landed near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023. This made India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first to land near the south pole. The mission was run by ISRO, the Indian Space Research Organisation.

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometres. It measures distance, not time. The nearest star to the Sun, Proxima Centauri, is about 4.24 light-years away.

The Milky Way is a spiral galaxy containing roughly 100 to 400 billion stars. Our Sun sits in one of its spiral arms about 26,000 light-years from the galactic centre, and it takes the Sun roughly 230 million years to orbit once.
