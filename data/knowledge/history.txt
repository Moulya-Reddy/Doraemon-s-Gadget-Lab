India became independent from British rule on 15 August 1947. The freedom movement included leaders such as Mahatma Gandhi, who promoted nonviolent resistance, and Jawaharlal Nehru became the first Prime Minister. Independence came together with the Partition of British India into India and Pakistan.

Johannes Gutenberg built the first European printing press with movable metal type around 1440 in Mainz, Germany. It made books cheaper and faster to produce, helping ideas, literacy and science spread across Europe.

The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the late 1700s. Steam engines, mechanised textile mills and iron production moved work from homes and farms into factories and caused rapid growth of cities.

The pyramids of Giza in Egypt were built around 2600 to 2500 BCE as tombs for pharaohs. The Great Pyramid was built for Pharaoh Khufu and was the tallest human-made structure in the world for nearly 4,000 years.

The Indus Valley Civilization flourished from about 2600 to 1900 BCE in what is now Pakistan and north-west India. Cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro had planned streets, brick houses and advanced drainage systems.

World War II lasted from 1939 to 1945 and was fought between the Allies and the Axis powers. Germany surrendered in May 1945, and Japan surrendered in September 1945 after which the war ended.

The internet grew from ARPANET, a research network first connected in 1969. In 1989 Tim Berners-Lee at CERN proposed the World Wide Web, which combined hyperlinks, web pages and browsers and made the internet easy for ordinary people to use.

The Mughal Empire in India began in 1526 when Babur won the Battle of Panipat. Akbar expanded and organised the empire, and Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra between 1632 and 1653 in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal.
